# Your website and the new NMC advertising rules — summary for Dr. Das

India's National Medical Commission has issued new guidelines on how doctors and clinics may present themselves online. In short: a clinic website may state facts — who you are, your qualifications and registration number, what you treat, your timings, fees and how to reach you — but it may not advertise. No patient praise, no "best/leading" claims, no counts of patients or procedures, no discounts, and no marketing partner named on the site.

We audited the new Medcare website against these rules. The good news: the site was built carefully and most of it already complies. Your registration number is shown on every page, there are no patient testimonials or star ratings, no prices or offers, health articles carry a clear disclaimer, and the booking form asks for consent with a proper privacy policy behind it.

## What we found and will change

**Five things need fixing:**

1. **"Built & Marketed by Automize Media Labs" in the footer.** The new rules say a doctor may not publicly engage a third party for marketing. We will remove this line (a plain "Website: …" credit is a possible, though less safe, alternative — we recommend removing it).
2. **"More than 10,000 echocardiography examinations."** The rules prohibit publishing procedure or patient counts, even when true. We will remove the number — your fellowship, memberships and published research carry the same authority and are all verifiable.
3. **Your photo in the website header and in link-preview images.** A doctor's photo used next to "Book appointment" buttons, or in the card shown when a page is shared on WhatsApp, counts as promotional publicity under the rules. One plain photo on your profile page is fine and stays; we will swap the other placements for clinic images.
4. **A Google star-rating feature that is built but not yet switched on.** Showing your Google rating on your own website is not permitted. We will remove the feature before it can appear.
5. **Sentences promoting the in-house pharmacy and naming the lab partner** (e.g. "sells genuine medicines", "you can buy it at the clinic", "lab tie-up with Agilus"). We will replace these with neutral wording: the pharmacy exists and its hours may be stated, but the site should not urge patients to buy from it, and partner brands should not be named.

**Four smaller items:** the phrase "recognised by the Heart Failure Association of India" needs the actual HFAI letter or listing to stay on the site (otherwise we reword it); we need to confirm consent is on file for the patient partly visible in the consultation photo and echo-room video, and that the video shows no patient name on the machine screen; the echo-screen image may be a mock-up rather than a real photo of your machine and needs checking; and the Google Maps box on the contact page should load only after consent or be labelled in the privacy policy.

**Three minor wording touches:** "advanced echocardiography lab" becomes "echocardiography lab with strain imaging"; "experienced physician" becomes "physician"; two small typos on the echo page get fixed.

## What we need from you

- The HFAI recognition document (if it exists) — or tell us to reword it.
- Confirmation that written consent is on file for everyone visible in the site photos and the echo-room video.
- Whether `echo-screen.jpg` is a real photo of your machine or a supplied illustration.
- Nothing else — the rest are edits on our side.

## What stays as it is

Your registration details, qualifications, memberships, publications and talks, the Health Library articles, the booking form, clinic photos, timings, address and the medical disclaimers all comply and stay unchanged.

None of the changes reduce how well the site works for patients — they remove promotion and keep the facts, which is exactly what the guidelines ask for.
