GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of medicines used for type 2 diabetes and, in some cases, weight management. They copy a natural gut hormone that helps control blood sugar and appetite.

**Quick answer**
- GLP-1 medicines increase insulin release when sugar is high, reduce the sugar released by the liver, slow stomach emptying and lower appetite.
- This can improve blood sugar and help with weight loss.
- They are prescription medicines with side effects, so they need medical supervision.

### How they work

- Stimulate insulin only when blood sugar is raised
- Reduce glucagon, which raises sugar
- Slow digestion so you feel full longer
- Act on appetite centres in the brain

### Who May benefit

- People with type 2 diabetes who need better control
- Those with diabetes and overweight or obesity
- Some people with heart disease risk, as chosen by their doctor

### How they are given

Most are injections, given once weekly or once daily, and one form is taken as a tablet. Your doctor will explain the technique and dose.

### Common side effects

- Nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea, often early on
- Constipation
- Reduced appetite
- Stomach discomfort

Eating smaller meals and increasing the dose slowly can help.

### Who should be careful

People with a history of pancreatitis, certain thyroid tumours or severe stomach problems may not be suitable. Tell your doctor your full history.

### Not a shortcut

These medicines work best with healthy eating and activity. Weight may return if treatment is stopped without lifestyle change.

### Key takeaways

- GLP-1 medicines lower sugar and appetite.
- They can help weight loss in many users.
- Nausea is the most common side effect.
- They are not suitable for everyone.
- They need medical guidance.

### Final thoughts

GLP-1 medicines are a useful option for some people, but the right choice depends on your health.


### FAQs

1. **Are GLP-1 medicines only for weight loss?**
   No. They were developed for type 2 diabetes, and some are also approved for weight management.
2. **Will I regain weight after stopping?**
   Many people do regain some weight, which is why lifestyle habits matter.
3. **Do they cause low blood sugar?**
   On their own the risk is low, but it can rise when combined with insulin or certain tablets.
4. **How long before I see results?**
   Blood sugar may improve within weeks, while weight change is gradual.
5. **Can I take them with metformin?**
   Yes, they are often used together. Your doctor will decide the combination.
6. **Are they safe for the heart?**
   Some have shown heart benefits in people with diabetes and heart risk. Your doctor will advise what suits you.
