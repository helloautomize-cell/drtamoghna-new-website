**Quick answer**
- HbA1c is usually checked every three months if your diabetes is not well controlled or treatment has changed, and about every six months once your sugar is stable.
- Home blood sugar checks depend on your treatment: people on insulin test more often, while those on tablets alone may test less often.
- Your doctor sets the plan.

### HbA1c: the three-month average

HbA1c reflects your average blood sugar over the past two to three months. Because it changes slowly, testing more often than every three months rarely adds value unless treatment has just changed.

- **Newly diagnosed or treatment changed:** about every three months.
- **Stable and at target:** about every six months.

### Home blood sugar checks

- **On insulin:** several checks a day, usually before meals and at bedtime, as advised.
- **On tablets with stable control:** a few checks a week, or fasting and after-meal checks on selected days.
- **During illness, a new medicine or lifestyle change:** test more often.
- **Pregnancy or planning pregnancy:** closer monitoring as advised by the doctor.

### When to test extra

- Symptoms of low sugar: shakiness, sweating, confusion, hunger.
- Symptoms of high sugar: excess thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision.
- Before driving if you are prone to lows.
- When sick, with fever, vomiting or infection.

### Making the numbers useful

Write down readings with time and meals, or use a meter or app. Share the log at each visit. Patterns matter more than a single number.

### When HbA1c may mislead

Anaemia, recent blood loss or transfusion, and some haemoglobin conditions can make HbA1c unreliable. Your doctor may use other tests in such cases.

### Key takeaways

- HbA1c is checked every three to six months.
- Home testing frequency depends on treatment and control.
- Test more during illness or when symptoms appear.
- Keep a log and review it with your doctor.

### FAQs

1. **Can I test HbA1c at any time of day?**
   Yes. HbA1c does not need fasting.
2. **Is monthly HbA1c necessary?**
   Usually not, unless your doctor wants to check a treatment change early.
3. **How many times a day should I check sugar on tablets?**
   It varies. Your doctor will guide the frequency.
4. **Which is better, fasting or after-meal testing?**
   Both give useful information.
5. **What if my home readings and HbA1c do not match?**
   Share both with your doctor. Different tests measure different things.
6. **Do I need a CGM?**
   Not everyone. Your doctor can advise.
