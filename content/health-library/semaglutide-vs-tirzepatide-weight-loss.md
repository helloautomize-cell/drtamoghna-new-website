Semaglutide and tirzepatide are prescription medicines used for type 2 diabetes and, in specific formulations, for weight management. Semaglutide acts on the GLP-1 hormone pathway. Tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP pathways. Both reduce appetite, slow stomach emptying and improve blood sugar. Clinical trials show both can lead to significant weight loss, with tirzepatide producing greater average weight loss in direct comparison studies. The right choice depends on your health, goals, tolerance and access.

**Quick answer**
- Both medicines can help with weight loss and sugar control when used with lifestyle changes.
- Tirzepatide has shown greater average weight loss in head-to-head trials, while semaglutide has strong evidence for heart protection in certain patients.
- Both commonly cause nausea and other digestive side effects.
- They need a prescription and medical supervision, and they are not suitable for everyone.

### How do they work?

#### Semaglutide

Mimics GLP-1, a gut hormone that increases fullness, slows digestion and helps the body release insulin when sugar is high.

#### Tirzepatide

Acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. This dual action may produce stronger effects on appetite and sugar control in many people.

### How do they compare?

#### Weight loss

Both produce meaningful weight loss on average. Tirzepatide has shown greater average weight loss in trials that compared them directly, but individual results vary.

#### Blood sugar

Both lower HbA1c significantly in type 2 diabetes.

#### Heart and kidney evidence

Semaglutide has shown cardiovascular benefits in people with established heart disease. Research on tirzepatide's long-term heart outcomes is ongoing.

#### How they are taken

Both are commonly given as weekly injections. An oral form of semaglutide is also available for diabetes.

### Common side effects

- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhoea or constipation
- Reduced appetite
- Stomach discomfort or bloating
- Tiredness

Side effects are often worse when starting or increasing the dose and usually settle over time.

### Less common but important risks

- Gallbladder problems
- Pancreatitis, with severe abdominal pain
- Dehydration from vomiting or diarrhoea
- Low blood sugar when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas

### Who should avoid them?

- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Those with a history of severe pancreatitis
- People with certain severe digestive conditions

### Important points

- Use only with a doctor's prescription and follow-up
- Avoid unregulated or compounded products sold online
- Combine with diet, activity and behaviour changes
- Weight may return if the medicine is stopped without lifestyle support

### Key takeaways

- Both act on gut hormone pathways.
- Tirzepatide acts on GLP-1 and GIP.
- Both cause meaningful weight loss on average.
- Digestive side effects are common at first.
- Medical supervision is essential.

### Final thoughts

These medicines have changed how obesity and diabetes are treated, but they work best as part of a complete care plan. A doctor can help you choose safely.


### FAQs

1. **Which is better, semaglutide or tirzepatide?**
   Neither is best for everyone. Tirzepatide has shown greater average weight loss in direct trials, while semaglutide has strong heart outcome data in certain patients. Your doctor will consider your health, goals and tolerance.
2. **Are these medicines only for diabetes?**
   Both have formulations approved for type 2 diabetes and, in many regions, for weight management in people with obesity or overweight with health conditions. Use depends on local approvals and your doctor's judgement.
3. **How quickly do they work for weight loss?**
   Weight loss usually starts within weeks and continues gradually over months as the dose is increased. Results vary based on dose, diet, activity and individual response.
4. **What are the most common side effects?**
   Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation and reduced appetite are most common, especially early on. Eating smaller meals and avoiding fatty foods may help. Report severe or ongoing symptoms.
5. **Can I buy these medicines online without a prescription?**
   No. These are prescription medicines that need medical supervision. Products sold without a prescription may be fake or unsafe. Always get them through a licensed pharmacy.
6. **Will I regain weight if I stop?**
   Many people regain some weight after stopping. Continuing healthy habits and discussing a long-term plan with your doctor help reduce regain.
