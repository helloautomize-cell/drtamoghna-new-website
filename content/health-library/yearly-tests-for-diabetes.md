**Quick answer**
- If you have diabetes, a yearly review should include HbA1c (more often if needed), a kidney check (urine albumin and serum creatinine), a lipid profile, an eye examination, a foot examination, and blood pressure and weight checks.
- An ECG or heart assessment is added based on your risk.

### Core Yearly Tests

**1. HbA1c**

Checked every three to six months, it shows how well your sugar has been controlled.

**2. Kidney tests**

Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and serum creatinine (eGFR) detect early kidney damage, often before symptoms appear.

**3. Lipid profile**

Cholesterol and triglycerides help assess heart risk.

**4. Dilated eye examination**

Detects diabetic retinopathy early, when treatment works best.

**5. Foot examination**

Checks sensation, circulation and skin to prevent ulcers.

**6. Blood pressure**

Measured at every visit.

**7. Weight and waist measurement**

Helps track metabolic health.

### Other Checks Based on Your Situation

- ECG or echocardiogram if you have heart risk factors.
- Liver function tests, since fatty liver is common in diabetes.
- Thyroid tests, especially in type 1 diabetes or if symptoms are present.
- Vitamin B12 levels in people on long-term metformin.
- Dental check-up.
- Vaccinations as advised, such as flu and pneumococcal.

### How to Prepare

Carry your previous reports and sugar log. Wear comfortable footwear that is easy to remove. Ask your doctor what each result means and what the target is.

### Key Takeaways

- Yearly checks catch complications early.
- Kidney, eye, foot and heart checks are essential.
- Extra tests depend on your health and medicines.
- Keep a record of all results.

### FAQs

1. **Do I need an eye test even if I see well?**
   Yes. Retinopathy can be present without symptoms.
2. **Why test urine in diabetes?**
   Albumin in urine is an early sign of kidney damage.
3. **How often is the foot examination needed?**
   At least once a year, and more often if there is nerve damage or poor circulation.
4. **Is a lipid profile needed every year?**
   Usually yes, or as your doctor advises.
5. **Do I need an ECG every year?**
   It depends on your age and risk factors.
6. **Can these tests be done on the same day?**
   Often yes, and clinics can group them for convenience.
