If you have diabetes, a small cut or blister on the foot can quickly become a serious problem. Some common home habits, such as applying harsh chemicals, cutting calluses yourself or ignoring the wound, can slow healing and increase infection risk. The safest approach is to clean the wound gently, cover it and see a doctor promptly.

**Quick answer**
- Never cut corns or calluses yourself, use blades or harsh chemicals, apply turmeric pastes or other home remedies directly to open wounds, soak feet in hot water, walk on the wound, ignore a wound that is not healing, or use tight bandages.
- Clean gently with saline or clean water, cover with a sterile dressing and see a doctor within a day or two, or the same day if there are signs of infection.

### Things you should never do

#### Do not cut corns or calluses

Using blades, scissors or corn caps can cause deep wounds and infection. Let a doctor or podiatrist manage them.

#### Do not apply harsh chemicals

Strong antiseptics, acids, kerosene or spirit can damage tissue and delay healing.

#### Do not apply home pastes to open wounds

Turmeric, toothpaste, ash, cow dung or herbal mixtures can introduce infection or hide worsening signs.

#### Do not soak your feet in hot water

Numb feet may not sense heat, leading to burns. Long soaking can also soften skin and slow healing.

#### Do not walk barefoot or keep walking on the wound

Pressure prevents healing. Rest the foot and use protective footwear.

#### Do not pop blisters

An intact blister protects the skin underneath. Cover it with a clean dressing.

#### Do not use tight bandages or tape directly on skin

These can reduce circulation or tear fragile skin.

#### Do not ignore it

Even small wounds can worsen quickly in diabetes.

#### Do not stop diabetes medicines

Good sugar control helps healing. Continue your medicines and monitor sugar closely.

### Safe first steps

- Wash your hands
- Gently clean the wound with saline or clean, lukewarm water
- Pat dry with a clean cloth
- Cover with a sterile, non-stick dressing
- Keep pressure off the foot
- Check the wound daily
- See a doctor within one to two days

### When to seek same-Day care

- Redness spreading around the wound
- Swelling or warmth
- Pus or bad smell
- Fever or chills
- Black or dark skin
- Pain in a foot that is usually numb
- Rising blood sugar levels

### What will the doctor do?

The doctor will examine the wound, check circulation and sensation, clean the area, remove dead tissue if needed and may prescribe antibiotics. X-rays or other tests may be done to check for deeper infection. Off-loading footwear may be advised.

### Key takeaways

- Never cut corns or calluses yourself.
- Avoid harsh chemicals and home pastes on wounds.
- Do not soak feet in hot water.
- Clean gently, cover and rest the foot.
- See a doctor quickly, especially with infection signs.

### Final thoughts

In diabetes, the right action in the first few days can prevent a small wound from becoming a serious infection. When in doubt, get it checked.


### FAQs

1. **Can I apply turmeric on a diabetic foot wound?**
   It is best not to apply turmeric or other home pastes directly to an open diabetic wound. They may introduce germs or hide signs of infection. Use a clean dressing and see a doctor.
2. **Should I use Dettol or spirit on a foot wound?**
   Strong antiseptics can damage healthy tissue and slow healing. Gently clean with saline or clean water and use products recommended by your doctor.
3. **Is it safe to remove a callus at home?**
   No. Cutting or scraping calluses at home can cause deep wounds and infection. A doctor or podiatrist should trim calluses safely.
4. **How do I know if my foot wound is infected?**
   Signs include spreading redness, swelling, warmth, pus, a bad smell, fever or worsening pain. Seek medical care the same day if you notice any of these.
5. **Should I keep walking if I have a foot wound?**
   Try to reduce pressure on the wound. Walking on it can slow healing. Your doctor may advise special footwear or rest to protect the area.
6. **How long should a diabetic foot wound take to heal?**
   Healing depends on size, depth, circulation and sugar control. Small wounds may heal in a few weeks with proper care. If there is no improvement within a few days, see a doctor.
