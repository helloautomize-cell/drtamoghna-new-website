H1: Metformin Side Effects: What Is Common and When to Call a Doctor

Metformin is often the first medicine prescribed for type 2 diabetes. Most people tolerate it well, but some notice side effects, especially at the start.

**Quick answer**
The most common side effects are stomach related, such as nausea, loose stools, bloating and a metallic taste. These often settle with time and can be reduced by taking metformin with food or using an extended-release form. Rare but serious problems, such as lactic acidosis, need urgent care.

### Common Side Effects

- Nausea or reduced appetite
- Loose stools or diarrhoea
- Gas and bloating
- Stomach discomfort
- A metallic taste

### How to Reduce Them

- Take with or right after meals
- Start at a low dose and increase slowly, as your doctor advises
- Ask about an extended-release version
- Avoid skipping meals

### Vitamin B12

Long-term use can lower vitamin B12 in some people, which may cause tingling or numbness in the feet. Your doctor may check B12 periodically.

### When to Call a Doctor

- Severe or persistent vomiting or diarrhoea
- Marked weakness, fast breathing or severe muscle pain
- Unusual drowsiness or stomach pain
- Signs of dehydration

### Situations Needing Caution

Kidney problems, severe infection, dehydration, heavy alcohol use and contrast dye scans may need metformin to be paused. Tell your doctor before such procedures.

### Does Metformin Harm the Kidneys?

Metformin does not damage kidneys in itself, but dose adjustment or stopping may be needed if kidney function is reduced.

### Key Takeaways

- Stomach upset is the most common issue.
- Taking it with food helps.
- Check B12 levels over time.
- Serious reactions are rare but need urgent care.
- Do not stop it without medical advice.

### FAQs

1. **Can metformin cause weight loss?**
   Some people lose a small amount of weight, mainly through reduced appetite, but results vary.
2. **How long do side effects last?**
   Stomach symptoms often improve within a few weeks.
3. **Should I take metformin before or after food?**
   Usually with or right after meals to reduce stomach upset.
4. **Is long-term use safe?**
   It has been used for many years and is generally considered safe with regular monitoring.
5. **Can I drink alcohol on metformin?**
   Heavy drinking raises the risk of complications. Discuss limits with your doctor.
6. **What if I miss a dose?**
   Take it with the next meal if remembered soon, but do not double the dose.
