Many people ask whether diabetes can be cured. Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition, but in some people blood sugar can return to a non-diabetic range. Doctors usually call this remission rather than cure.

**Quick answer**
- Remission means blood sugar stays in a normal range for a sustained period without diabetes medicines.
- It is more likely when diabetes is recent and significant weight is lost.
- It is not a permanent cure, because sugar can rise again, so regular follow-up is still needed.

### What Does Remission Mean?

Remission is commonly described as an HbA1c below the diabetes range, maintained for at least three months after stopping glucose-lowering medicines. The underlying tendency toward diabetes remains.

### Who Is More Likely to Achieve It?

- People diagnosed recently
- Those who can lose a meaningful amount of weight
- People with good remaining insulin production
- Those who can sustain lifestyle changes

### What Helps

- Gradual, sustained weight loss under medical guidance
- A balanced diet with controlled portions and fewer refined carbohydrates
- Regular physical activity, including walking and strength work
- Good sleep and stress management
- In selected cases, medicines or other medical weight loss options chosen by your doctor

### What Remission Does Not Mean

- You can return to old habits
- You no longer need check-ups
- Complications risk is gone

Eye, kidney and foot checks should continue.

### Safety First

Never stop diabetes medicines on your own. Doses must be reduced step by step under medical supervision to avoid dangerous swings in sugar.

### Key Takeaways

- Remission is possible for some, especially early in the disease.
- Weight loss is the strongest factor.
- It is not a cure, and sugar can rise again.
- Do not stop medicines without medical advice.
- Regular monitoring continues even after remission.

### FAQs

1. **Is diabetes reversal the same as a cure?**
   No. Reversal or remission means sugar is controlled without medicines, but the tendency can return.
2. **How much weight loss is needed?**
   It varies by person. Larger and sustained weight loss improves the chance, and your doctor can set a target for you.
3. **Can type 1 diabetes go into remission this way?**
   No. Type 1 diabetes is caused by loss of insulin-producing cells and always needs insulin.
4. **How long does diabetes have to be present for remission to be possible?**
   Remission is more likely when diabetes is recent, but some people with longer-standing disease also improve. This is individual.
5. **Do I still need blood tests in remission?**
   Yes. Periodic HbA1c and annual eye, kidney and foot checks help catch any return of high sugar.
6. **Can crash diets reverse diabetes?**
   Extreme diets can be unsafe, especially with diabetes medicines. Any structured weight loss plan should be supervised by a doctor.
