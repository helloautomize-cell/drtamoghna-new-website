Prediabetes can move toward diabetes without warning symptoms, so regular testing is the way to stay ahead of it.

**Quick answer**
- Most people with prediabetes are advised to have blood sugar tested at least once a year.
- Those at higher risk, or who are making changes to lower their sugar, may be tested more often, sometimes every three to six months.
- Your doctor will set the schedule.

### Which Tests Are Used

- HbA1c
- Fasting blood sugar
- Post-meal or glucose tolerance test in some cases

### When to Test More Often

- Rising weight or waist size
- Strong family history of diabetes
- History of gestational diabetes
- High blood pressure or cholesterol
- New symptoms like thirst or frequent urination
- Starting a new lifestyle plan, to check progress

### What Else Should Be Checked

- Blood pressure
- Cholesterol and triglycerides
- Weight and waist measurement
- Kidney and liver tests if advised

### Keep a Simple Record

Note your reports with dates so trends are easy to see. A rising trend is a signal to act sooner.

### Key Takeaways

- Test at least once a year.
- High-risk people may need more frequent checks.
- HbA1c and fasting sugar are the usual tests.
- Check blood pressure and cholesterol too.
- Track results over time.

### FAQs

1. **Is yearly testing enough for prediabetes?**
   For many people yes, but some need more frequent checks. Follow your doctor's advice.
2. **Can I test sugar at home?**
   A home glucometer can give readings, but diagnosis and HbA1c tracking are done through lab tests.
3. **Should I test if I feel fine?**
   Yes. Prediabetes usually causes no symptoms.
4. **What if my result becomes normal?**
   Continue periodic testing, as sugar can rise again.
5. **What if my result moves into the diabetes range?**
   Your doctor will confirm it and discuss treatment and lifestyle steps.
6. **Do children and teens need testing?**
   Those who are overweight with other risk factors may be advised testing. Discuss this with a doctor.
