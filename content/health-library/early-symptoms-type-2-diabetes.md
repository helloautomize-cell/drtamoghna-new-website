Type 2 diabetes can develop slowly over years. Many people feel fine at first, and the condition is often found only on a routine blood test. When symptoms do appear, they are easy to put down to age, stress or a busy life.

**Quick answer**
- Common early symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing cuts and repeated infections.
- Some people notice tingling in the feet or darkened skin folds on the neck.
- Many have no symptoms at all, so testing is the only reliable way to know.

### Common Early Symptoms

- Feeling thirsty more often than usual
- Passing urine frequently, including at night
- Unusual tiredness
- Blurred vision that comes and goes
- Cuts or sores that heal slowly
- Repeated skin, gum or urinary infections
- Tingling or numbness in the hands or feet
- Dark, velvety patches of skin on the neck or underarms
- Weight loss without trying, in some people

### Why Symptoms Are Often Missed

Blood sugar can rise gradually, and the body adapts. Symptoms may be mild for a long time. This is why people with risk factors should be tested even when they feel well.

### Who Should Get Tested

- Adults with a family history of diabetes
- People who are overweight, especially with a larger waistline
- Those with high blood pressure or high cholesterol
- Women who had gestational diabetes
- Anyone with the symptoms listed above

### How Is Diabetes Confirmed?

Your doctor may use a fasting blood sugar, an HbA1c test or a post-meal sugar test. A repeat test is often needed to confirm the result.

### What to Do If You Notice Symptoms

Do not wait for symptoms to get worse. See a doctor, get tested and start lifestyle changes early. Early control protects the eyes, kidneys, nerves and heart.

### Key Takeaways

- Early type 2 diabetes may cause no symptoms.
- Thirst, frequent urination and tiredness are common signs.
- Slow-healing wounds and blurred vision need a check.
- Risk factors justify regular testing.
- Early treatment prevents complications.

### FAQs

1. **Can you have type 2 diabetes and feel completely normal?**
   Yes. Many people have no symptoms for years, which is why screening matters when you have risk factors.
2. **Why does diabetes cause frequent urination?**
   When blood sugar is high, the kidneys try to remove the extra sugar through urine. This pulls water with it, so you pass urine more often and feel thirsty.
3. **Does blurred vision mean I have diabetes?**
   Not always, but high blood sugar can change the shape of the eye lens and blur vision. An eye check and a blood sugar test can help find the cause.
4. **What are dark patches on the neck?**
   A darkening of skin folds called acanthosis nigricans can be linked to insulin resistance. It is worth getting your blood sugar checked.
5. **At what age should I start testing?**
   Adults are commonly advised to begin screening around the age of 35, and earlier if they have risk factors. Your doctor can advise on timing.
6. **Can early diabetes be controlled without medicines?**
   Some people can bring blood sugar down with diet, activity and weight loss, but this depends on the individual. Your doctor will guide the plan.
