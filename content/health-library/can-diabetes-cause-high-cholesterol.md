H1: Can Diabetes Cause High Cholesterol?

Yes. Diabetes, especially type 2 diabetes with insulin resistance, changes how the liver and body process fats. This often leads to high triglycerides, low HDL ("good") cholesterol and more harmful LDL particles. This pattern, called diabetic dyslipidaemia, raises the risk of heart disease. Good sugar control and lipid treatment work together to lower that risk.

**Quick answer**
Insulin resistance makes the liver release more fat into the blood, raising triglycerides and lowering HDL. LDL particles become smaller and denser, which makes them more likely to damage arteries. Poor sugar control worsens these changes. Lifestyle changes, better glucose control and often statins help manage cholesterol in diabetes.

### How Does Diabetes Affect Cholesterol?

#### More fat released by the liver

When insulin does not work well, the liver produces more triglyceride-rich particles.

#### Lower HDL

HDL helps remove cholesterol from arteries. Insulin resistance tends to lower HDL.

#### Smaller, denser LDL

Even if LDL numbers look normal, the particles may be more harmful.

#### Poor sugar control

High blood sugar further raises triglycerides and affects lipid balance.

### Which Pattern Is Common in Diabetes?

- High triglycerides
- Low HDL cholesterol
- Normal or mildly high LDL, but more harmful particles
- High non-HDL cholesterol

### Does Type 1 Diabetes Also Affect Cholesterol?

Well-controlled type 1 diabetes may have near-normal lipid levels. Poor control, kidney disease or weight gain can lead to abnormal lipids.

### Other Factors That Worsen Cholesterol

- Excess belly fat
- Inactivity
- Diets high in refined carbs and fried foods
- Alcohol
- Smoking
- Hypothyroidism
- Kidney disease
- Some medicines

### How Can You Improve Cholesterol With Diabetes?

#### Control blood sugar

Better HbA1c often lowers triglycerides.

#### Eat for heart health

Reduce sugar, refined carbs, fried foods and trans fats. Add vegetables, pulses, whole grains and nuts.

#### Move more

Regular activity raises HDL and lowers triglycerides.

#### Lose excess weight

Even modest weight loss helps.

#### Stop smoking

Smoking lowers HDL and damages blood vessels.

#### Use medicines when advised

Statins are commonly prescribed for people with diabetes to lower LDL and heart risk. Other medicines may be added if needed.

### How Often Should Lipids Be Checked?

Most people with diabetes should have a lipid profile at least once a year, or more often when treatment is being adjusted.

### Key Takeaways

- Diabetes changes how the body handles fats.
- High TG and low HDL are common.
- LDL particles may be more harmful in diabetes.
- Sugar control improves lipid levels.
- Statins are often advised for heart protection.

### Final Thoughts

Managing cholesterol is as important as managing sugar when you have diabetes. Treating both together gives the best protection for your heart. ### FAQs

1. **Why do people with diabetes have high triglycerides?**
   Insulin resistance causes the liver to release more fat into the blood. Poor sugar control and refined carbs worsen this. Better glucose control often lowers triglycerides.
2. **Can controlling sugar lower cholesterol?**
   It mainly helps lower triglycerides and may improve HDL slightly. LDL often needs separate treatment, such as statins, especially in people with diabetes.
3. **Should everyone with diabetes take a statin?**
   Many adults with diabetes benefit from statins, especially those over 40 or with other risk factors. The decision depends on age and overall risk. Your doctor will guide you.
4. **Is diabetic dyslipidaemia dangerous?**
   Yes. This lipid pattern increases the risk of heart attack and stroke. Treating it along with sugar and BP control significantly lowers this risk.
5. **Can weight loss improve cholesterol in diabetes?**
   Yes. Losing excess weight, especially belly fat, lowers triglycerides and can raise HDL. It also improves sugar control and blood pressure.
6. **How often should cholesterol be checked if I have diabetes?**
   At least once a year, or more often if you are starting or changing treatment. Your doctor will set the best schedule for you.
