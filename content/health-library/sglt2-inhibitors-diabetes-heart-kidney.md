SGLT2 inhibitors are a group of diabetes medicines that lower blood sugar by helping the kidneys remove extra glucose through urine. Beyond sugar control, some medicines in this group have shown benefits for heart failure and chronic kidney disease in suitable patients. That is why doctors increasingly consider them for people with type 2 diabetes who also have heart or kidney risk.

**Quick answer**
- SGLT2 inhibitors work in the kidneys to flush out excess sugar.
- They may also lower the risk of heart failure hospitalisation and slow kidney disease progression in selected people.
- Common side effects include genital infections and increased urination.
- They are not suitable for everyone, so your doctor decides based on your kidney function, heart health and other medicines.

### How do SGLT2 inhibitors work?

Normally, the kidneys filter glucose from the blood and reabsorb most of it. SGLT2 is a protein that helps with this reabsorption. Blocking it lets more glucose leave the body in urine, which lowers blood sugar.

### What are the possible benefits?

- Lower blood sugar and HbA1c
- Modest weight loss in many people
- Small reduction in blood pressure
- Reduced risk of heart failure hospitalisation in suitable patients
- Slower progression of chronic kidney disease in suitable patients
- Low risk of hypoglycaemia when used without insulin or sulfonylureas

### Who May be considered for them?

- People with type 2 diabetes and heart failure
- Those with chronic kidney disease
- People with established heart disease or high cardiovascular risk
- Those who need additional sugar control

Some are also used in heart failure or kidney disease without diabetes, under specialist guidance.

### What are the common side effects?

- Genital fungal infections, especially in women
- Increased urination and thirst
- Urinary tract infections
- Dehydration or dizziness, especially in older adults or with diuretics

### What rare but serious risks should you know?

#### Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

A rare condition that can occur even with near-normal sugar levels. Symptoms include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, tiredness and breathing difficulty. Seek urgent care if these occur.

#### Severe infections

Rarely, serious genital infections can occur. Report pain, redness or swelling promptly.

### Practical safety tips

- Drink adequate water unless told otherwise
- Maintain genital hygiene
- Ask your doctor about pausing the medicine during acute illness, fasting, vomiting or before surgery
- Do not stop or change the medicine without medical advice

### Key takeaways

- SGLT2 inhibitors lower sugar through the kidneys.
- Some offer heart and kidney protection.
- Genital infections are a common side effect.
- DKA is rare but important to recognise.
- Your doctor decides if they suit you.

### Final thoughts

SGLT2 inhibitors show how modern diabetes care looks beyond sugar numbers to protect the heart and kidneys. The right choice depends on your full health picture.


### FAQs

1. **Do SGLT2 inhibitors cause low blood sugar?**
   On their own, they have a low risk of hypoglycaemia. The risk rises when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas. Your doctor may adjust other medicines to keep sugar levels safe.
2. **Why do SGLT2 inhibitors cause urinary infections?**
   They increase sugar in the urine, which can encourage germ growth. Good hygiene and enough water help lower the risk. Report burning, itching or discharge to your doctor.
3. **Can SGLT2 inhibitors protect the kidneys?**
   Some medicines in this group have been shown to slow kidney disease progression in suitable patients. Your doctor will check kidney function before and during treatment.
4. **Are SGLT2 inhibitors safe for older adults?**
   They can be used in many older adults, but dehydration and dizziness are more likely. Doctors monitor kidney function, blood pressure and fluid intake carefully in this age group.
5. **Should I stop SGLT2 inhibitors when I am sick?**
   During illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, poor eating or before surgery, doctors often advise pausing them temporarily. Ask your doctor for clear sick-day rules before starting.
6. **Do SGLT2 inhibitors help with weight loss?**
   Many people lose a small amount of weight because calories are lost through urine. They are not a primary weight-loss medicine, but the effect can be helpful.
