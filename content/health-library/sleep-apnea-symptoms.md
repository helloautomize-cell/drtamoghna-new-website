H1: Sleep Apnea Symptoms: Sleepiness, Headaches and Gasping

Obstructive sleep apnea causes symptoms both at night and during the day. At night, people may snore loudly, stop breathing for short periods and wake up gasping. During the day, they often feel very sleepy, wake with headaches and struggle to concentrate. Many people do not realise they have it because the night-time symptoms happen while they are asleep.

**Quick answer**
Common sleep apnea symptoms include loud snoring, breathing pauses, gasping or choking at night, frequent night urination, morning headaches, dry mouth, excessive daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, irritability and high blood pressure that is hard to control. If you have several of these, ask your doctor about a sleep study.

### Night-Time Symptoms

- Loud, regular snoring
- Pauses in breathing noticed by a partner
- Waking up gasping or choking
- Restless sleep and tossing
- Night sweats
- Waking often to urinate
- Dry mouth or sore throat on waking

### Daytime Symptoms

- Excessive sleepiness during the day
- Falling asleep while reading, watching TV or driving
- Morning headaches
- Poor concentration and memory
- Irritability or low mood
- Low energy and motivation
- Reduced sex drive

### Symptoms in Women

Women may have less obvious snoring and more symptoms like insomnia, fatigue, headaches and mood changes. Sleep apnea in women is often missed.

### Linked Health Problems

- High blood pressure, especially if hard to control
- Type 2 diabetes or poor sugar control
- Irregular heartbeat such as atrial fibrillation
- Heart failure
- Obesity
- Fatty liver

### Why Do These Symptoms Happen?

When the airway closes during sleep, oxygen levels drop and the brain briefly wakes you to restart breathing. This can happen many times an hour, preventing deep, restful sleep. The repeated drops in oxygen and stress responses also strain the heart and blood vessels.

### When Is It Urgent?

Falling asleep while driving or during important tasks is dangerous. If this happens, avoid driving and seek medical advice promptly.

### What Should You Do?

- Note your symptoms and ask your partner about night-time observations
- See a doctor for screening
- Ask about a sleep study
- Avoid alcohol and sedatives before bed
- Work on weight management if needed

### Key Takeaways

- Sleep apnea has night and daytime symptoms.
- Gasping and breathing pauses are key night signs.
- Daytime sleepiness and morning headaches are common.
- Women may have less typical symptoms.
- A sleep study confirms the diagnosis.

Feeling tired all the time is not something you have to accept. Recognising sleep apnea symptoms can lead to treatment that restores energy and protects your heart. ### FAQs

1. **What is the most common sign of sleep apnea?**
   Loud snoring with pauses in breathing is the most common sign. Daytime sleepiness is another key symptom. Many people are unaware of night-time symptoms until a partner notices.
2. **Can sleep apnea cause morning headaches?**
   Yes. Low oxygen and high carbon dioxide levels during the night can cause headaches on waking. These often improve once sleep apnea is treated.
3. **Why do I wake up often to urinate at night?**
   Sleep apnea can trigger hormonal changes that increase night-time urine production. Frequent night urination can be a sign of sleep apnea, though other causes should also be checked.
4. **Is it dangerous to feel sleepy while driving?**
   Yes. Drowsy driving increases the risk of accidents. If you feel sleepy while driving, stop safely and seek medical advice about possible sleep apnea.
5. **Can sleep apnea affect mood?**
   Yes. Poor sleep can cause irritability, low mood and anxiety. Many people notice better mood and concentration after treatment.
6. **Do women get sleep apnea?**
   Yes. Women can have sleep apnea, especially after menopause or with obesity. Symptoms may be less typical, such as fatigue, insomnia or headaches, so it is often missed.
