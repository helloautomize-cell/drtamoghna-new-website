### Intro (answer-first)

Diabetes is a long-term condition in which blood sugar stays higher than normal because the body does not make enough insulin or cannot use it well. At Medcare in North Bongaigaon, Dr. Tamoghna Das looks after adults and young people with type 1, type 2 and other types of diabetes, with the aim of keeping sugar in a safe range and preventing damage to the heart, kidneys, eyes, nerves and feet. HbA1c, CGM, foot checks, ECG and echo are done in the clinic.

### At a glance

- **What it is:** high blood sugar over a long time, often called "sugar" in everyday speech
- **How common:** about 1 in 9 adults in India has diabetes, and many more have prediabetes (ICMR-INDIAB study, 2023)
- **Main tests:** fasting sugar, sugar after food, HbA1c
- **Diagnosis (adults, not pregnant):** fasting sugar 126 mg/dL or more, or HbA1c 6.5% or more, confirmed on repeat testing
- **At Medcare:** consultation, HbA1c, finger-prick sugar, CGM, diabetic foot check, body composition, ECG, echo
- **Goal of care:** steady sugar, fewer highs and lows, and no complications

### Types of diabetes we look after

- **Type 2 diabetes.** The most common type. The body still makes insulin but does not respond to it well. It is linked to family history, extra weight around the waist, low activity, poor sleep and stress. People of South Asian origin often develop it at a younger age and a lower body weight.
- **Type 1 diabetes.** The body makes very little or no insulin, so insulin injections are needed from the start. It usually begins in childhood or early adulthood but can start at any age.
- **Diabetes in pregnancy.** High sugar first found in pregnancy (gestational diabetes) or diabetes that was present before pregnancy. See [Diabetes in pregnancy](/diabetes-care/diabetes-in-pregnancy/).
- **Monogenic and other types.** Rarer forms caused by a single gene change, by pancreas disease, or by medicines such as steroids. These sometimes need a different treatment, so finding the type matters.
- **Prediabetes.** Sugar higher than normal but below the diabetes level (fasting 100 to 125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4%). This is the ideal time to act, because changes now can delay or prevent diabetes.

### Signs that can mean high sugar

Many people have no symptoms for years. When symptoms do appear, they can include:
- Passing urine often, especially at night
- Feeling very thirsty
- Tiredness, weakness
- Weight loss without trying
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing cuts, frequent skin, gum or urine infections
- Tingling or burning in the feet

### What happens at Medcare

1. **Detailed history.** Your symptoms, family history, food habits, work hours, sleep, other illnesses and every medicine you take.
2. **Examination.** Weight, waist, BP, pulse, feet, skin and signs of complications.
3. **Tests in the clinic.** HbA1c and finger-prick sugar during the visit. A diabetic foot check for nerve feeling and blood flow. ECG and echo when your heart needs a look, which is common in people who have had diabetes for some years.
4. **Blood and urine tests.** Usually kidney function, urine albumin, cholesterol and others as needed. Samples are collected at Medcare through our lab partner Agilus, and reports are given at the clinic.
5. **Your plan.** Medicines with clear timings, a food plan built around what you eat at home, an activity goal, and a schedule for sugar checks.
6. **Complication screening.** A yearly plan for eyes, kidneys, feet and heart, so problems are found while they are still small.

Dr. Das decides which tests you need after he has examined you, so you only do the tests that help.

### Treatment, in plain words

Treatment is different for each person. It usually includes:
- **Food:** regular meals, smaller rice portions, more vegetables, dal and protein, fewer sweets and sweet drinks. We work with your usual meals rather than giving a foreign diet chart.
- **Activity:** most adults benefit from about 150 minutes of brisk walking or similar activity a week, spread over most days, plus some muscle-strengthening work.
- **Medicines:** tablets, injections or insulin, chosen for your type of diabetes, your kidney and heart health, your weight and your budget.
- **Monitoring:** home sugar checks or CGM, and HbA1c every 3 months until stable.
- **Protection:** BP and cholesterol control, foot care, and vaccines as advised.

### How to prepare for your visit

- Bring your medicines (or strips), your sugar meter or diary and reports from the last year
- If a fasting test may be needed, ask us before the visit how to manage your morning dose
- Write down any low sugar episodes: time, reading and what you were doing

### After your visit

You leave with written medicine timings, sugar targets, food and activity advice, and a follow-up date. Most people are reviewed every 1 to 3 months until sugar is steady, then every 3 to 6 months. You can WhatsApp sugar readings and reports between visits.

### When to see a doctor urgently

Callout:
> Call 108 or 112, or go to a hospital emergency, if a person with diabetes has: confusion, fainting or a seizure; sugar below 70 mg/dL that does not come up after taking sugar; sugar above 300 mg/dL with vomiting, deep fast breathing or drowsiness; chest pain or breathlessness; or a foot that is suddenly red, hot, swollen or turning black.

### FAQs

1. **Can diabetes be cured?**
   Type 1 diabetes cannot be cured at present. Type 2 diabetes is long-term, but with weight loss, food changes and activity, some people bring their sugar into the normal range and need fewer medicines. This needs regular checks, because sugar can rise again. Be careful of anyone promising a permanent cure or "insulin-free" treatment.
2. **What is a good HbA1c?**
   For many adults with diabetes the target is below 7%, but it depends on age, other illnesses and the risk of low sugar. An older person who lives alone may have a slightly higher target to stay safe. Dr. Das will set your personal target and explain it.
3. **Once I start insulin, do I have to take it for life?**
   Not always. People with type 1 diabetes need insulin for life. In type 2 diabetes, insulin is sometimes started to bring very high sugar down and later reduced or stopped when other treatment works. Insulin is not a sign of failure. It is one of the safest and most effective ways to control sugar.
4. **Can I eat rice if I have diabetes?**
   Yes, in sensible amounts. Fill half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with dal, fish, egg, paneer or meat, and a quarter with rice or roti. Eating vegetables and protein first and walking after meals also helps. Dr. Das will help you plan meals around foods you enjoy.
5. **Why does a diabetes doctor check my heart?**
   Diabetes raises the risk of heart attack and heart failure, sometimes without the usual warning symptoms. An ECG and echo, including strain imaging, can show early changes in heart function. Finding these early means BP, cholesterol and sugar can be treated more firmly to protect the heart.
6. **How often should I check my sugar at home?**
   It depends on your treatment. People on insulin often check several times a day or use CGM. People on tablets alone may check a few times a week, at different times. Dr. Das will tell you when to check and what to do with the numbers.
7. **Is diabetes in young people the same as in older adults?**
   Not always. Young people can have type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes or a rarer monogenic type. The right treatment depends on the type, so Dr. Das looks carefully at age, weight, family history and test results before deciding.
